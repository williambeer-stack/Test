Take your cut before the money lands.

Hybrid Split Settlement sits on top of bank acquiring rails. Each store keeps its own MID. A split engine takes out HQ royalties and onseller fees, then sends each party's share straight to its own BSB. No one holds merchant funds, and no one has to invoice.

Where the hybrid model sits

Legacy model

Bank acquirer · 1 MID = 1 BSB

  • Merchant account 1:1
  • MID onboarding, disbursement & fees
  • Competitive pricing
  • Customers work around it with direct debits or master account pooling

Local + global

Hybrid model Whitespace

Bank acquirer + processor orchestration

  • Merchant account 1:1, franchisee MIDs kept
  • Processor runs the disbursement & fee engine
  • Low compliance & operational burden for HQ
  • Direct IC++ rates kept

Australia-specific · too low-yield for global PayFacs · too complex for bank mainframes

Platform / marketplace

PayFac or PayFac-as-a-service

  • Master merchant with sub-merchants
  • Platform handles onboarding, disbursement & fees
  • High compliance risk for HQ
  • Expensive

Global · built for digital marketplaces, not QSR or franchise
